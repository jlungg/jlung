Overview

Small, real, finishable work.

Freelancer.com wanted university students to take action on the platform: hire a freelancer for a passion project, or offer their own skills. The brief also asked to encourage early entrepreneurship, side hustles and creative alternatives to the 9-to-5.

The answer was to stop selling freelancing as a career and reframe it as a side quest: work students can start this week, alongside or before a career. A silent pixel knight, two student characters and a five-stage campaign carry the idea from a campus sighting to a first job and a first review.

Platform audit · Freelancer.com

The mechanics fit. The voice didn’t.

The platform already works in ways that suit students. The problem is who it looks like it’s for.

What works
  • Clients post a job and freelancers bid, so talent comes to you
  • Contests: see finished options before choosing
  • Small budgets suit student wallets
  • Payment held until the work is approved
Pain points
  • No TikTok presence
  • Legacy website with a corporate tone
  • Skewed to experienced freelancers: beginners with no reviews lose bids
  • Nothing speaks to students yet

Chapter 03

The strategy

Position Freelancer as the side quest platform.

The answer
Position Freelancer as the side quest platform: small, real, finishable work students can start this week, alongside or before a career.

Campaign line

Hire-side

Find a freelancer

For students with a passion project: a band, a brand, a club. You don’t need a company to hire someone. Post the job, freelancers bid, you pick.

Earn-side

Unlock Starter Quests

For students with a skill but no reviews. Starter Quests are small jobs only students can bid on, so their first real job comes before their first real career.

The face

Frankee Lancerlot

A silent knight who could be any student. Frankee gets the attention, on campus and on social, then points to both sides of the platform.

Tone of voice

Absurd in situation, never in claims.

What we are

  • Dry and deadpan
  • Plain-spoken: one job, one price
  • Absurd in situation, never in claims
  • On the student’s side

What we aren’t

  • Hustle-bro or “build your empire”
  • Anti-career or anti-uni
  • Full of brand-made slang
  • Promising income or replies

Rule of thumb

  • The 8-bit visuals carry the theme
  • The copy stays plain, using game words only where the campaign needs them (Side Quests, Starter Quests, New Quest Unlocked)

Creative direction

A knight for hire, back for the student era.

The characters · The face

Meet Frankee Lancerlot.

Who is Frankee?

Silent, faceless and gender-neutral, so anyone can be Frankee.

Catchphrase

“I’m Frankee Lancerlot, and I like to freelance a lot.”

Rules

Never speaks or shows their face, no pronouns in copy, humour at no one’s expense.

Personality

Deadpan, serious about absurd tasks, mysterious, camera-aware. Imagine the Stig’s silence with Deadpool’s cheekiness.

“I’m Frankee Lancerlot, and I like to freelance a lot.”

Brand guidelines · Campaign lockup

A pixel crest with Freelancer’s hummingbirds.

The parts

  • Pixel crest: shield, crown and two hummingbirds, a nod to Freelancer’s logo
  • Headline: “Careers later. Side quests now.” in blackletter
  • Always with the Freelancer logo on the same piece

Usage

  • Use the full lockup on covers, story headers and end cards
  • Use the crest alone at small sizes (avatars, app icon, notifications)
  • Keep clear space of one crest-width around the lockup
  • Don’t recolour, stretch or redraw the crest
